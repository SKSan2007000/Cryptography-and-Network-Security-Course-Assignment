Security Audit Drill

A short viva-style module for explaining design decisions to a reviewer. It is deliberately tied to the implemented system rather than generic interview questions.

Q1. Why is a hash alone insufficient for source authentication?
Anyone who can alter the document can also recompute an unkeyed hash. A digital signature binds the digest to a private signing key and allows verification with the trusted public key.
Q2. What exactly happens after the diagnosis is altered?
Canonical bytes change, the recomputed digest changes, the stored digest no longer matches, and verification of the signature over the changed digest fails.
Q3. Why does the prototype use both ECDSA-P256 and Ed25519?
They expose a meaningful trade-off: ECDSA is widely represented in established certificate ecosystems, while Ed25519 offers compact keys/signatures and a simple signing interface. The final choice depends on interoperability and trust infrastructure.
Q4. Why is the key fingerprint stored?
The fingerprint gives a compact identifier for the public verification key. In production, it would be bound to a certificate or institutional trust registry and paired with revocation and rotation.
Q5. What is not solved by this prototype?
Signatures do not provide confidentiality, secure transport, full identity proofing, clinical safety, key recovery, revocation infrastructure or regulatory compliance by themselves.
